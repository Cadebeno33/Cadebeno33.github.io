Recent Work

Spaghetti Plot of a Hurricane Approximately 5 Days from Landfall

Using MATLAB, I developed a simulation to model the potential paths of hurricanes influenced by constantly changing atmospheric factors. At each time step, a random variable is introduced to account for uncertainty and generate varying trajectories. The model is structured around the strength of the Bermuda High, with several parameters held constant to ensure stability while still allowing for adaptability. This framework enables the study of hurricane paths under a wide range of initial conditions, making it a versatile tool for exploring different scenarios.

Matlab Code: Hurricane Trajectory

In this code, I defined a function to simulate a hurricane’s path based on the wind field strength of the Bermuda High, with variability introduced through a random term (ε). While the screenshot highlights the trajectory function, the full project also includes a histogram of probable landfall positions along the Florida coast. For those interested in a deeper look, I have prepared a detailed report in LaTeX that I would be glad to share.

Ice Thickness Prediction on a Lake

This plot illustrates the growth of ice on Lake Mendota, modeled in MATLAB using a variety of environmental factors. The simulation is initialized once the lake is fully covered in ice, which typically occurs after 7–10 consecutive days of below-freezing temperatures. From that point, the model predicts ice thickness by incorporating meteorological data, snowfall, and other relevant variables, with a focus on small- to medium-sized lakes. This approach enables accurate forecasts of ice thickness several days in advance, offering practical applications for safety, research, and environmental monitoring. A more detailed discussion of the model and its implications can be found in my accompanying LaTeX report, which I would be glad to share.

Matlab Code: Ice Formation

This code incorporates factors such as snowfall events, air–water temperature alignment, and daily updates to ice thickness. By defining parameters, setting objectives, and running the model, I was able to generate accurate predictions based on forecasted weather data. With further development, the model could be linked to external weather services to provide real-time, automated forecasts of ice growth. Such a tool has valuable applications in lake studies, recreational planning, and public safety.
